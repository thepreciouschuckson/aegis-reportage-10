The Federal Government has placed 46 people exposed to Nigeria's Ebola patient on a 21-day no-fly list.

The Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, directed the Nigerian Civil Aviation Authority (NCAA) to inform local and international airlines of the restriction.

The order followed a virtual meeting on Wednesday between Keyamo and the Coordinating Minister of Health and Social Welfare, Prof. Muhammad Ali Pate. They discussed ways to contain the country's first confirmed Ebola case since 2014, according to a statement from Keyamo, [Punch reported](https://punchng.com/ebola-fg-orders-46-exposed-persons-on-21-day-no-fly-list/).

Laboratory tests confirmed on October 20 that the patient has the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola virus disease.

The [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int) (WHO) said the woman recently travelled from Uganda to Nigeria. Her symptoms began on October 7, four days after she arrived.

She sought treatment at a private health facility in Abuja on October 11, according to the [Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention](https://ncdc.gov.ng) (NCDC). She was first treated for malaria-like symptoms, but her condition did not improve.

Pate earlier said the patient is in isolation and receiving medical care. Health officials are expanding contact tracing and other containment measures.
