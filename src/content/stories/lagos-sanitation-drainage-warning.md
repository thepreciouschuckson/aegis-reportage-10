The Lagos State Government has renewed its call for proper waste disposal. Officials say residents in several communities are dumping refuse into drainage channels, which raises the risk of flooding. [The government made the warning](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/11/drainage-lagos-govt-warns-against-indiscriminate-refuse-dumping/) after the October edition of the state's monthly environmental sanitation exercise.

During the exercise, officials saw improper waste disposal that can block drains.

The state's Head of Service, Bode Agoro, said government efforts to improve sanitation will achieve limited results without the cooperation of residents. He said the October exercise was the seventh monthly sanitation exercise since March 2026. He urged local government chairmen and community leaders to step up public awareness campaigns on keeping surroundings clean.

Agoro said officials saw residents throwing refuse into canals even as the government works to clear blocked drains. He said the Ministry of the Environment has been told about the affected canals. The ministry is arranging to clear them and may rebuild some of the drainage infrastructure.

He said environmental cleanliness is not the government's responsibility alone. Residents must take part in keeping their surroundings healthy.

The Chairman of Ikeja Local Government, Akeem Dauda, said the council continues to educate residents about the dangers of indiscriminate waste disposal and the need to protect drains. The Ikeja exercise covered Onilekere Street, Saidu Street, Olumo Street, Ijesha Close and Olukoyi Street.

A similar exercise took place in Ikorodu. The wife of the Lagos State Governor, Dr Ibijoke Sanwo-Olu, joined activities there to promote environmental responsibility.

The government urged residents to dispose of waste properly, keep their surroundings clean and support efforts to prevent flooding and improve public health.
