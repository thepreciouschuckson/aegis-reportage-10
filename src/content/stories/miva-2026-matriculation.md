[Miva Open University](https://miva.edu.ng) has matriculated 12,260 new students and announced plans to expand technology-driven education and reach one million African learners over the next decade.

Chancellor Sim Shagaya made the announcement at the university's 2026 matriculation ceremony in Abuja. He said more than 40,000 students have enrolled since the university began operating in 2023.

Shagaya said Miva is working to set up study centres in every local government area in Nigeria. He also announced plans for robotics and drone laboratories and a research institution.

He said the one-million target aims to widen access to education while keeping academic standards. He said technology will make quality education more accessible to Nigerians and other Africans.

Shagaya said Miva was set up to remove barriers that keep people out of higher education, including work commitments, family responsibilities, financial difficulty and conventional admission systems, The Guardian Nigeria reported.

He warned new students that the flexibility of online study does not make a degree easier to earn.

He said the university is committed to rigorous academic standards, accessible lecturers, learning centres and technology support. Miva also provides an AI learning companion to help students study.

Vice-Chancellor Prof. Tayo Arulogun reaffirmed the university's commitment to flexible, technology-driven learning. He said the growing student population reflects rising demand for alternative routes to a university degree, especially among Nigerians who balance study with work, business and family.

The keynote speaker, [World Health Organization](https://www.who.int) Assistant Director-General Dr Chikwe Ihekweazu, urged students to use their education to challenge injustice, demand accountability and serve society.

Ihekweazu also warned students against using artificial intelligence to get a qualification without gaining the knowledge and skills the education is meant to give. He urged them to use AI as a learning tool and not as a way to avoid academic work.
