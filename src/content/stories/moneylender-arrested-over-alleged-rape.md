The Federal Capital Territory (FCT) Police Command has arrested Usman Mohammed over allegations of raping, sexually assaulting and blackmailing women in Abuja. Police say the victims include widows and other financially vulnerable women who owed him money.

FCT Police Public Relations Officer SP Josephine Adeh confirmed the arrest on Wednesday, November 11. She said the suspect is accused of exploitative money-lending practices that target vulnerable women.

Police say the suspect lured one of his borrowers into his vehicle and drove her to an isolated spot in Lugbe, Abuja. They allege he raped and sexually assaulted her there.

Adeh alleged that the suspect forced the woman to undress and recorded her naked. Police say he then threatened to expose the recording and to kill her if she failed to meet his repayment demands.

The woman had borrowed ₦600,000 from the suspect and was expected to pay ₦30,000 weekly as interest, according to reports. She reportedly missed a payment after running into financial difficulty.

She reported the incident at the Lugbe Police Division on November 6. Officers then began an investigation to find the suspect.

Police traced him to the Federal Housing Authority (FHA) area of Lugbe. They say he tried to run when he saw the officers, but they chased and arrested him before he reached the expressway, [The Guardian reported](https://guardian.ng/news/nigeria/metro/fct-police-arrest-suspect-for-rape-cyberbullying-sexual-assault-of-women-in-abuja/).

At a police parade, the suspect reportedly admitted lending the woman money, demanding weekly interest, sexually assaulting her and recording her naked.

The command said he remains in custody and is being questioned further. He is expected to face charges in court when the investigation ends.

FCT Commissioner of Police CP Ahmed Muhammed Sanusi condemned sexual exploitation, intimidation, violence and cyberbullying. He urged residents, especially vulnerable people, to report abuse, threats and blackmail at the nearest police station and not suffer in silence.

The case has renewed concern about the exploitation of financially vulnerable women and the need for stronger protection against sexual violence and financial abuse.
