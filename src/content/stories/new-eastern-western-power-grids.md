The Federal Government has approved plans to build new eastern and western electricity grids. The move aims to reduce Nigeria's recurring grid collapses and strengthen its transmission network.

Minister of Power Joseph Tegbe announced the plan at an [energy conference in Lagos](https://fmino.gov.ng/?p=58678). He said the government is preparing to begin procurement for the projects. He said Nigerians could see benefits within two to three years.

Tegbe also said the government is running a nationwide technical audit of electricity infrastructure. The audit will identify faults and decide which facilities need urgent repair. He said it will make sure government funds go to the real causes of failures and not to the wrong equipment.

He named ageing transmission infrastructure as one challenge. Some transmission towers and lines have been in service for decades.

The minister said Nigeria has been divided into three energy zones to help the government prioritise investment. He singled out the Lagos and Abuja corridors as areas with major electricity demand.

The Federal Ministry of Power has said earlier that it is investing in measures to stabilise the national grid and reduce collapses and voltage fluctuations. The new eastern and western grids are expected to add to these efforts by increasing the capacity and resilience of the transmission network.

The projects are still at the planning and procurement stage. Their impact will depend on procurement, funding, construction and implementation.

The announcement comes amid long-standing concern about unreliable power supply and repeated grid disruptions.
