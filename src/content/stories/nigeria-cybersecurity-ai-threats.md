The Nigerian government is stepping up efforts to protect citizens, businesses and digital infrastructure from emerging threats linked to artificial intelligence (AI).

The Director-General of the [National Information Technology Development Agency](https://nitda.gov.ng) (NITDA), Kashifu Inuwa Abdullahi, called for stronger cybersecurity measures at the second International Cybersecurity Conference 2026 in Abuja.

The conference theme was "Cyber Resilience in the Age of AI: Defending Minds, Networks and Nations." Government officials, lawmakers, cybersecurity experts and industry leaders met to discuss growing digital risks, Voice of Nigeria reported.

Abdullahi said cybersecurity should go beyond preventing attacks. It should also help individuals, organisations and governments recover from breaches and adapt to new threats.

He said cybercriminals increasingly use AI to create fake videos, manipulate voices and spread misleading information. This makes it harder for internet users to tell genuine content from manipulated content. He urged public awareness efforts and stronger measures to help Nigerians spot false information and protect themselves from online deception.

The Executive Vice Chairman of the Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC), Aminu Maida, warned that AI-powered attacks could threaten Nigeria's telecommunications infrastructure and digital economy. Maida was represented by the NCC's Executive Commissioner for Technical Services, Abraham Oshadami.

The NCC called for closer cooperation among telecom operators, financial institutions, regulators and security agencies, [the News Agency of Nigeria reported](https://nannews.ng/?p=347229). It also called for better systems that help telecom networks respond to attacks.

The Attorney-General of the Federation, Lateef Fagbemi, said the government is working to strengthen Nigeria's cybercrime laws while protecting citizens' fundamental rights. The proposed reforms are expected to cover new technologies, electronic evidence and the investigation of cybercrime.

More Nigerians now use online banking, social media and e-commerce. Protecting personal information is therefore key to public trust in digital services.

The conference called for continued collaboration, public education and stronger cybersecurity policies.
