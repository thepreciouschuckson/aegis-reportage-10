Nigeria has been elected to the United Nations Human Rights Council for the 2027 to 2029 term. It won 172 of the 181 valid votes cast by the UN General Assembly, [the federal government said](https://fmino.gov.ng/nigeria-elected-to-the-human-rights-council-for-the-2027-2029-term/).

The election took place on October 13, 2026. Nigeria was one of four countries elected from the African Group. Zambia received 177 votes, Ghana 173, Nigeria 172 and Burundi 160.

Nigeria begins its three-year term on January 1, 2027, alongside 14 other newly elected members of the 47-member Council.

The [Human Rights Council](https://www.ohchr.org/en/hrbodies/hrc/home), set up in 2006, is the UN's main intergovernmental body for promoting and protecting human rights worldwide. Its members take part in discussions and initiatives on human rights violations and fundamental freedoms.

In a statement on October 14, Nigeria's Ministry of Foreign Affairs said the result reflects UN member states' confidence in Nigeria's commitment to promoting and protecting human rights. The ministry said Nigeria will use its seat to promote constructive dialogue, multilateral cooperation, human dignity, justice and the protection of fundamental freedoms.

Membership also brings closer international attention to Nigeria's own human rights record. Council members take part in global discussions and must also face scrutiny of human rights issues at home and abroad.

Nigeria's last term on the Council ended in 2020. The country announced earlier this year that it wanted another seat.
